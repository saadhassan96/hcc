Human-Centered Computing Group

We build and study computing systems with people in mind: tools that make communication and information accessible, visualizations that explain data and models, and imaging methods for science and medicine.

Co-directed by

  • RF
    Rebecca Faust
  • SH
    Saad Hassan
  • BS
    Brian Summa

The Human-Centered Computing (HCC) Group brings together three faculty in the Department of Computer Science at Tulane University, in New Orleans, with their students and staff. We work across human-computer interaction, accessibility, visualization, computer graphics, and applied AI, often with partners in medicine, education, and local government.

Research

Accessible data visualization

Saad Hassan and Rebecca Faust

Charts and data representations that people with disabilities can explore, from haptic encodings on smartphones to accessible personal health visualizations, grounded in a review of the field.

Haptics, Personal health data, Scoping review

1 publication

Funding Tulane University, NSF

Civic and community-engaged AI

Saad Hassan

Participatory approaches to AI that connect residents, communities, and local governments, make public services easier to reach, and examine how tools such as patient-facing chatbots affect health disparities.

Participatory design, Public services, Health equity

3 publications

Funding NSF, Tulane University

Explainable projections and semantic interaction

Rebecca Faust

Methods that explain dimensionality reduction and deep learning projections, and let people steer them by moving points, grouping examples, or describing what they see in natural language.

Dimensionality reduction, Semantic interaction, Large language models

12 publications

Funding CRA / NSF Computing Innovation Fellows

Imaging for cancer surgery and pathology

Brian Summa

Software that turns gigantic microscopy scans of removed tissue into fast, navigable 3D maps of tumor margins, and variable-resolution slide imaging that records how pathologists review a slide.

MAGIC-SCAN, PathCAM, Medical image processing

11 publications

Funding ARPA-H, NIH

Reading support tools

Saad Hassan

Customizable text simplification and in-context reading supports for people who are deaf or hard of hearing, people with low English literacy, and other readers who face barriers with complex text.

Text simplification, Large language models, Literature reviews

2 publications

Funding Tulane University

Sign language and captioning

Saad Hassan

Tools that help people learn, look up, and understand American Sign Language, and richer captions that carry emotion, sound, and other non-speech information for d/Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers.

ASL learning, Sign lookup, Affective captions, Haptics

12 publications

Funding Tulane University, Louisiana Board of Regents, Duolingo

Topology, large data, and machine learning

Brian Summa

Topological data analysis and learning for comparing complex scalar fields, tools that read and steer the internal states of deep models such as diffusion transformers and language models, and fast access to massive simulation and image data.

Merge trees, Model interpretability, Large scientific data

14 publications

Funding U.S. Department of Energy

Visualizing data science code

Rebecca Faust

Systems that trace what an analysis script does as it runs and show the values, versions, and choices behind a result, so analysts can debug, compare, and explain their work.

Program traces, Computational notebooks, Debugging

5 publications
